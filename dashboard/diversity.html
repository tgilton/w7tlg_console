Phase Coherence — RX1 vs RX2

The RSPduo's two tuners share one ADC sample clock in Dual Tuner mode, so their IQ streams are guaranteed sample-rate-locked. Whether their own downconversion local oscillators also stay phase-locked to each other — not just the shared clock — is a separate question this scope answers empirically, by watching the phase difference between RX1's and RX2's tuned-frequency FFT bin over time.

The dot's absolute position doesn't mean anything on its own (it includes whatever arbitrary offset each receiver's capture happened to start at). What matters is whether it holds still (the two tuners are phase-locked — real antenna/propagation differences will make it wander slowly, that's expected) or spins continuously (the LOs are free-running relative to each other, at whatever their frequency difference is).

Most meaningful with a real, roughly-equal-strength signal tuned on both receivers — on noise alone the dot will look random regardless of coherence, since there's nothing correlated between the two antennas to measure a phase relationship against.

RX0 — Combined Signal

RX0 = RX1 − z·RX2, where z = gain·ej·phase is the weight below — a real, listenable third signal, not just a meter. With the splitter feeding both receivers the same antenna, gain=1 / phase matching the coherence scope's own reading above should null it hard. On two different antennas, sweep for whichever combination pulls a signal furthest out of the noise, or knocks a specific noise source down.

This costs real CPU (a third full demod pipeline) — off by default, only runs while enabled below.
